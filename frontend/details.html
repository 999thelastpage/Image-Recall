Recall finds the photo you remember, even if one detail is wrong.

This page uses simple words on purpose. It follows the ASD-STE100 idea: short sentences, common words, one idea in each sentence. Move your mouse over the boxes. Each box tells you more.

1. The problem

You remember a photo. One detail in your memory is wrong. You remember a red bus, but the bus is blue. Normal search trusts all your words. It searches for a red bus. It does not find your photo.

Recall works in a different way. Recall thinks: one detail can be wrong. Recall tests your description again and again. Each time, Recall removes one detail. Then Recall asks you small questions. At the end, Recall shows the photo and shows the proof.

3. The main trick: remove one fact at a time

Your description has facts. In this example, the facts are A, B, C, D. Recall does not make one search. Recall makes many searches. Each search removes a different fact. Move over a row to see why.

ALL FACTSA thingB colourC countD place
REMOVE AA thingB colourC countD place
REMOVE BA thingB colourC countD place
REMOVE CA thingB colourC countD place
REMOVE DA thingB colourC countD place

Then Recall joins all the branch lists: the full search, one search per dropped clue, relaxed searches that keep the noun, and a few colour swaps. Your answers give more weight to the right lists. No list can delete a photo.

5. New: Find my proof

Sometimes you do not search for a memory. You search for proof. You know the photo exists. You need it for a claim, a deposit, or an argument. But you remember it a bit wrong: the wrong month, the wrong colour.

Proof Finder is Recall with one job: find that photo and prove it. The search is the same engine. The questions fix your wrong details. At the end, you tap Show the proof. Recall checks the top 3 photos and writes what is true: the thing, the count, the colour, the place.

Use it like this:
  1. On the home page, tap Find my proof.
  2. Write the proof photo from memory. One wrong detail is OK.
  3. Answer the questions. Tap Show the proof at the end.

Real life examples: the photo of the wall before the repair (insurance), the room on the day you moved in (deposit), the broken part before you fixed it (warranty). In this demo library, try the cake, the cat, and the tennis examples under Find my proof.

6. Use the tool: features and tests

Each test takes one or two minutes. Do the test. Check what you see. The demo library has 5,000 photos. On your own computer, you can also use the big library and your own folders.

Test 1: Compare both (see the difference)

Do: tap Compare both. Write a big purple bus parked in a parking spot. Tap Search my photos.

You see: two lists side by side. Simple search gives results in about 0.1 seconds. Recall needs some seconds: it splits your words first. Green marks show photos that only Recall found. The purple colour is wrong on purpose: the real bus is not purple.

Test 2: Ask me questions (the repair)

Do: tap Ask me questions. Write three red buses on a city street. Answer question 1: pick the colour detail. Then answer the next questions.

You see: the order of photos changes after each answer. Arrows show photos that moved up or down. Nothing is deleted. At most 3 questions.

Test 3: Find my proof

Do: tap Find my proof. Tap the example prove the cat did it. Answer the questions. At the end tap Show the proof (top 3 photos).

You see: simple proof lines for 3 photos: what Recall found, how many, and the facts it checked. This is the text you can show to another person.

Test 4: The AI double-check

Do: finish Test 2 or Test 3. Tap Double-check top 10 with AI. Wait about 15 seconds.

You see: Muse reads the top photos and makes a new order. Each photo shows its AI score out of 10.

Test 5: The secret target game (test like a judge)

Do: open the secret target picker. Pick one photo. Do not tell the tool. Go back and search for it from memory: make one detail wrong on purpose. Use Ask me questions.

You see: at the end, the tool tells you the place of your secret photo in the final top 10. The tool never knows your pick. The pick stays in your browser.

Test 6: Your own folder (only on your computer)

Do: on the home page, tap Choose folder… and pick a photo folder. Wait for the bar to finish. Then search your photos with words.

You see: a new library in the list at the top. Search works on your photos. Only small previews of about 23 KB and 3 KB of numbers per photo stay on disk. Your big originals do not move. This test is off in the public demo.

These numbers come from real tests that we ran on this computer.

Each case has one wrong detail, and the right photo is 1 of 5,000 photos. To pass, Recall must put that photo in its top 5, which is the top 0.1% of the library. Recall needs a maximum of 3 questions. A test program gives true answers to the questions.

Wrong thing (you remember a bus, but it is a truck): simple search finds 17 of 30, and Recall finds 22 of 30. Wrong colour: simple search finds 19 of 30, and Recall finds 21 of 30.

Speed: one search over 82,783 photos takes about 0.07 seconds. All Recall searches together take under 0.1 seconds. The Muse word step takes some seconds, and Recall keeps its answer: the same question a second time takes about 0.06 seconds in total.

Photo sets: 5,000 photos in the public demo. 82,783 photos in the big test set on this computer. Same engine, same numbers format (768 numbers per photo).

8. What is next: video, documents and more

People forget details everywhere, not only in photos. The Recall idea can work on any large collection: split the memory into facts, doubt one fact at a time, ask a small question, and show the proof.

Video is our next step. A video is many photos in a row. Recall takes the important pictures from the video, and searches them like photos. The answer is not a photo. The answer is a moment: “look at second 01:23”.

Photo search is real today. The areas below are plans, not built yet. Each one needs its own search model and its own proof check. Move your mouse over an area to see an example.

9. Where Recall is strong, and how it grows

Proven today, ready to grow: we built this prototype for one wrong detail, and our tests show that it works: Recall finds more photos than simple search (43 of 60 against 36 of 60). The next step is more engineering, so that Recall can also repair a memory with two or more wrong details.

Better models, better results: Recall is built so that its models can change. We expect better vision and reranking models to improve results further. We already saw this once: our last search model upgrade almost doubled the top-5 score of the first search on our big set, from 17 to 32 of 100.

Big libraries: Recall already runs on 82,783 photos. One search takes about 0.07 seconds, and all Recall searches together take under 0.1 seconds. It checks every photo, so no photo is skipped. This speed leaves room for more questions and deeper AI checks, and for larger libraries.

Proof you can check: the colour check reads the colour inside the SAM box, and strong shadows or coloured light can change the reading. So Recall shows you each reading next to what you said. When a check is not sure, the proof line says “can't tell either way”. Recall does not guess for you.
